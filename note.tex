\begin{abstract}
We give an exactly symmetric finite triangle-local distribution with
$P(A=B=C)=41/144>1/4$, addressing the exact-symmetry question posed by
B\"aumer, Gitton, Kriv\'achy, Gisin, and Renner. We also provide a certification
and interoperability layer for numerical local models. It exports independent
source laws and response tables as portable rational witnesses, checks locality
and squared residuals exactly, and adds a validated target enclosure to obtain
a rigorous upper bound $U$ on distance to the intended distribution.
Selected development witnesses approximate noisy EJM and Renou correlations
with $U$ between $6\times10^{-11}$ and $4\times10^{-8}$.
A separate CPU experiment compares triangle-local, the public LHV-Net 2020
architecture, and localmodels on identical target arrays, with ten independent
cold starts per method and target and 10- and 60-second budgets. Neural
checkpoints receive fresh held-out evaluation with uncertainty. The software
supports certification of numerical constructions without rerunning or trusting
their search procedure.
\end{abstract}

\section{An exactly symmetric triangle-local witness}
The triangle network has three independent sources $\alpha,\beta,\gamma$,
shared by $BC$, $CA$, and $AB$, respectively. Local responses depend on the two
sources incident to each party~\cite{fritz}. B\"aumer et al.~\cite{baumer}
ask whether a four-outcome triangle-local distribution with full party and
simultaneous outcome-relabeling symmetry can have $P(A=B=C)>1/4$.
Their discussion includes approximately symmetric neural examples near $0.29$.
The following construction gives exact symmetry and an exact probability.

Put $O=\{0,1,2,3\}$ and $S=\{(i,j)\in O^2:i\ne j\}$. Choose
$\alpha,\beta,\gamma$ independently and uniformly from the twelve elements of
$S$. For $x=(x_1,x_2)$ and $y=(y_1,y_2)$ define
\[
 f(x,y)=\begin{cases}x_2,&x_2\in\{y_1,y_2\},\\x_1,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}
 \qquad (A,B,C)=(f(\beta,\gamma),f(\gamma,\alpha),f(\alpha,\beta)).
\]
Each response is deterministic and uses precisely its incident sources, so
the resulting law is triangle-local. Enumerating the $12^3$ equally likely
source triples gives
\begin{equation}
 P(a,b,c)=\frac1{1728}
 \begin{cases}
 123,&|\{a,b,c\}|=1,\\
 19,&|\{a,b,c\}|=2,\\
 23,&|\{a,b,c\}|=3.
 \end{cases}\label{eq:symmetric}
\end{equation}
There are $4$, $36$, and $24$ output triples in these three classes, and
$4(123)+36(19)+24(23)=1728$. The formula depends only on the equality pattern;
hence it is invariant under all party permutations and all simultaneous
permutations of the four output labels. In particular,
\[
 P(A=B=C)=\frac{4(123)}{1728}=\frac{41}{144}=\frac14+\frac5{144}.
\]
This is an explicit answer to the existence question as stated in that paper,
with full symmetry established exactly.
The small integer enumeration and the complete finite response tables are
included with the certificate. A separate
\href{https://github.com/the-omega-institute/trureturing/blob/59c09d64d1050c396df54b58b4f410f09f0c25d0/D5/S3/Quantum/Entanglement/TriangleSymmetricLocalRefutation.lean}{revision-pinned Lean development}
records the continuous-source formulation; the computations reported here use
the independent integer checker.

\section{What a certificate establishes}
A finite model consists of rational probability vectors $q_\alpha,q_\beta,
q_\gamma$ and rational row-stochastic response tables $A_{jk}^a,B_{ki}^b,C_{ij}^c$.
Its distribution is
\begin{equation}
 Q(a,b,c)=\sum_{i,j,k}q_\alpha(i)q_\beta(j)q_\gamma(k)
 A_{jk}^a B_{ki}^b C_{ij}^c.\label{eq:model}
\end{equation}
Checking nonnegative entries and unit row sums establishes valid response
kernels and independent source laws. Clearing denominators in
\eqref{eq:model} reduces the full contraction to integer arithmetic. The
checker recomputes all probabilities, normalization, symmetry, $L_1$ error,
and squared Euclidean error; it does not trust an optimizer's success flag.
Finite-cardinality methods are established in network locality~\cite{rosset,dasilva}.
Here the deliverable is the exported, independently checkable object.

Write $P$ for the intended target and $\widetilde P$ for the rational target
stored with the witness. The checker computes
$E=\|\widetilde P-Q\|_2^2\in\mathbb Q_{\ge0}$ exactly. A separate interval
calculation supplies $\|P-\widetilde P\|_2\le\delta_{\rm target}$. Thus
\begin{equation}
 d_\triangle(P):=\inf_{R\in\mathcal L_\triangle}\|P-R\|_2
 \le\|P-Q\|_2\le\sqrt E+\delta_{\rm target}\le U.\label{eq:bound}
\end{equation}
Every displayed $U$ is rounded upward. The $\delta_{\rm target}$ values are
outward-rounded interval upper bounds. Separate high-precision point
evaluations are cross-checks, not the justification of those bounds. The
interval formulas use 100 decimal digits; their binary endpoints are converted
to exact rationals, followed by exact endpoint subtraction and an integer
ceiling for the square root.

If $E=0$, the stored target is local. Locality of the intended target follows
when target identification also establishes $P=\widetilde P$. A positive $U$
certifies a nearby local distribution, regardless of its size. A positive fit
residual supplies neither a nonlocality proof nor a lower bound on
$d_\triangle(P)$. The integer checker is tested software, not a formally
verified implementation. A second standard-library audit uses rational
variable elimination with a different contraction order and no package imports.

\subsection*{Target identity and noise placement}
Decimal parameters denote their exact decimal rationals: for example,
$u^2=0.85$ means $17/20$. Outputs use $a,b,c\in\{0,1,2,3\}$ with index
$16a+4b+c$. For EJM, the entries for one, two, and three distinct labels are
\[
 \frac1{256}(4+9v+9v^2+3v^3,\;4+v-3v^2-v^3,\;4-3v+3v^2+v^3)
\]
under independent source noise and
\[
 \frac1{256}(4+9v^2+12v^3,\;4+v^2-4v^3,\;4-3v^2+4v^3)
\]
under independent detector noise. Rational $v$ gives exact rational targets,
so their stored witnesses have $\delta_{\rm target}=0$.
For Renou~\cite{renou}, the outcome basis is
$|01\rangle,|10\rangle,u|00\rangle+\sqrt{1-u^2}|11\rangle,
\sqrt{1-u^2}|00\rangle-u|11\rangle$, in cyclic qubit order, with three
$|\phi^+\rangle=(|00\rangle+|11\rangle)/\sqrt2$ sources. For source
visibility $v$, each independent edge instead carries
\[
 \rho_v=v|\phi^+\rangle\langle\phi^+|+(1-v)I_4/4,
 \qquad \rho_{\rm network}=\rho_v^{\otimes3}.
\]
This is the source-noise convention in Table~\ref{tab:best}, including $v=0.89$.
Independent detector noise applies
$K_v^{\otimes3}$, where $K_v=vI+(1-v)J/4$; it is different from global
white-noise mixing. General Renou targets require algebraic enclosures.

\subsection*{Transport to lower detector visibility}
Let $0\le v_L\le v_H$ with $v_H>0$, and $r=v_L/v_H$. Applying $K_r$ to
each response row preserves locality and gives $Q_L=K_r^{\otimes3}Q_H$.
Since $K_rK_{v_H}=K_{v_L}$, and the eigenvalues of the symmetric channel $K_r$
are $1,r,r,r$, its Euclidean operator norm is one. Therefore
\[
 \|Q_L-P(v_L)\|_2\le\|Q_H-P(v_H)\|_2\le U_H.
\]
An exact high-visibility target model yields exact lower-visibility models;
an approximate one yields approximations with this controlled error.

\section{A matched cold-start comparison}
The experiment compares triangle-local, LHV-Net-2020~\cite{krivachy}, and
localmodels~\cite{dasilva} on three declared targets: EJM source $v=0.80$,
EJM detector $v=0.86$, and Renou detector $u^2=0.85$, $v=0.93$.
Each method receives the same stored 64-entry double-precision array $T$, with
its ordering, exact physical parameters and SHA-256 recorded. All reported
scores are recomputed in double precision against that array, including neural
outputs. The training loss may differ. In particular, localmodels reports
$\sqrt{\mathrm{SSE}/64}$ internally: we explicitly verify
\begin{equation}
 L_2=\sqrt{\mathrm{SSE}}=8\,\mathrm{RMSE}
\end{equation}
from every saved output, rather than reading its internal error field.

There are ten independent runs per method--target pair, each observed at
10 and 60 seconds. No method receives an analytic control, recognized target,
fitted seed, or neighboring checkpoint. The run order is deterministically
shuffled. One solver process runs at a time on an Apple M2 Pro CPU with
16 GiB memory and one numerical-library thread; GPUs are disabled.
The timing begins with initialization after loading the target and common
libraries. It includes model construction, optimization, and checkpoint
selection. Held-out evaluation, export, and certification are additional costs.

Both finite methods use seven symbols per source, with unrestricted stochastic
responses and free independent source weights. Triangle-local alternates
L-BFGS (400-iteration cap), convex polishing (120 sweeps, 120 inner steps), and
two perturbation searches, restarting if the cycle completes. It calls the
hybrid routine directly, bypassing target recognition. Localmodels uses its
public constrained trust-region optimizer, one random trial per call and zero
outer error tolerance, restarting on return. The benchmark retains the best
feasible output evaluated before each deadline; infeasible optimizer trial
points are excluded.

LHV-Net uses the pinned public response routing and architecture with three
hidden tanh layers of width 16, batch size 256, and Adadelta learning rate 2
on the KL loss. An eager TensorFlow wrapper replaces obsolete Keras generator
calls. This is a small CPU configuration of the public code, not a replication
of every architecture and training budget used in the 2020 paper. Neural
checkpoints minimize a fixed validation-set residual, with all validation
charged to search time. Final testing is separate, as detailed below.

For a fixed neural checkpoint let $X$ be its conditional-product probability
vector, $Q=\mathbb EX$, and $\widehat Q_N=N^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^N X_i$.
Fresh independent source triples give
\[
 \mathbb E\|\widehat Q_N-T\|_2^2
 =\|Q-T\|_2^2+
 \frac{\operatorname{tr}\operatorname{Cov}(X)}{N}.
\]
Each selected checkpoint receives $N=2^{20}$ fresh samples in 32 blocks,
after both checkpoint choices are frozen. These samples are independent of
training and validation and never select a checkpoint. Tables and Figure~2
report the raw score $s=\|\widehat Q_N-T\|_2$, without variance correction
or truncation. For the $B=32$ block means $\overline X_b$, we save
\[
 \widehat V=\frac{1}{B(B-1)}\sum_{b=1}^{B}
 \|\overline X_b-\widehat Q_N\|_2^2.
\]
The separate diagnostic $\sqrt{\max(0,s^2-\widehat V)}$ and estimated RMS
sampling error $\sqrt{\widehat V}$ are not the plotted score. Each of 2,000
bootstrap resamples draws $B$ block means with replacement and recomputes the
raw norm $s^*$. If $q_p$ is the empirical $p$-quantile of these norms
(NumPy's default linear interpolation), the displayed basic 95\% interval is
\[
 [\max(0,2s-q_{0.975}),\;\max(0,2s-q_{0.025})].
\]
Thus both interval endpoints are clipped at zero. These are approximate
statistical intervals, not rigorous enclosures. Finite-model outputs use full
contractions.

\section{Scope, context, and reproducibility}
The practical contribution is a certification and interoperability layer:
portable rational source distributions and response tables, rigorous accounting
for target approximation, compact representations, and a small checker.
LHV-Net already defines generative local models, and localmodels already
provides finite response tables. Both can supply numerical constructions for
this certificate pipeline. The finite-model benchmark exports from both finite
solvers are rationalized and independently checked after timing; the native
search comparison remains separate from that certificate-production cost.

\input{benchmarks/round4/extraction-prose.tex}

The numerical search uses full finite contractions and nonconvex optimization.
Fixing five factors makes the sixth factor's squared-loss subproblem convex,
but finite inner iteration caps remain part of the method. Recognizers, lifted
analytic seeds, and continuation are useful optional workflows whose preparation
costs must be accounted for. The earlier configuration study is descriptive:
its runs have different total budgets and do not isolate causal effects.

Kriv\'achy et al.'s fitted noise curves are historical context, not measured
baseline outcomes or certified lower bounds. The present CPU experiment does
not compare GPUs, long continuation workflows, or the adaptive PyTorch toolbox
of Girardin et al.~\cite{girardin}; it establishes no unrestricted
state-of-the-art optimizer ranking. Gitton and Renner's exact-arithmetic
nonclassicality proof for ideal EJM~\cite{gitton} is complementary to our
constructive upper bounds. An exclusion alone is not a numerical distance
lower bound. The unrestricted optimum is not determined here.

certificates, detector-channel composition, zero-weight trimming, and outward
rounding. The independent rational audit checks every selected witness and
every newly exported finite checkpoint. Raw distributions and target hashes
